# Example brief: Blueleaf sparkling water ad

> **Report rule:** the HTML report uses only VAI and its components (Reach, Speed, Hold). Raw metrics mentioned below (fixation ratio, TTFF, fixation time, counts) explain RealEye exports and must not appear in the report.


**Fictional example.** The creative, audience, and numbers are illustrative. This is not a RealEye study, an analysis of a customer's item, or a performance benchmark.

## Decision at a glance

**Keep the product image. Retest the claim's placement before approving the ad for short exposures.** The can was fixated by 20 of 24 people in the early window, but the "Zero sugar" claim was fixated by only 6 of 24. The brand team wants shoppers to notice both the brand and that benefit at a glance.

**Test context:** Blueleaf ad, V1. Grocery shoppers viewed a single static ad freely for 5 s on desktop with webcam tracking. The brief locates the can centrally, the brand mark at top left, and the claim at lower right. The primary window is 0.5-3 s from image onset.

## What we found

1. **The can reached most viewers in this sample.** 20/24 (83%) fixated it at least once by the early cutoff, versus 12/24 (50%) on the brand mark. Keep the product prominent. The comparison does not prove that the can drew attention away from the logo; its region is larger and more central.
2. **The claim's problem is early reach, not demonstrably slow pickup.** Only 6/24 (25%) fixated it at 0.5-3 s. Those six reached it in an average of 0.80 s from image onset; that does not make it the first element viewers generally looked at. By 5 s the claim reached 14/24 (58%) in the same cohort, eight more people. Leaving it in its current position is a risk for short exposures.
3. **A look is not message takeout.** The people who fixated on the claim early spent 0.65 s on it in total on average. This does not show that they read it or could recall it. There are no participant paths or message responses to verify the intended order or takeaway.

## What to change and how to verify it

Move the claim beside the can, outside the can and logo AOIs. Keep the text, size, contrast, and other elements unchanged so the retest isolates placement. Randomly assign first-time viewers to one version each. For this communication goal, decide by the share who correctly name **both** the brand and the benefit without prompting, with scoring and a worthwhile difference set before testing. Use 0.5-3 s claim Ratio to diagnose whether the edit changed attention, and monitor early logo and can Ratio as guardrails. V1 alone provides no winning version.

## Example selection: two toothpaste ads

**Fictional comparison.** Randomly assigned shoppers saw one version each. The planned primary outcome was correct unaided recall of both the brand and the whitening benefit, with a 10 percentage point minimum worthwhile gain. All assigned viewers answered the survey; gaze was usable for 100 of the 120 people per version.

| Version | Correct joint recall (primary) | Early pack fixation Ratio, 0.5-3 s (diagnostic) |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | 48/120 (40%) | 85/100 (85%) |
| B | 78/120 (65%) | 75/100 (75%) |

**Choose B for communication in this test.** Its joint recall was 25 percentage points higher (approximate 95% independent-proportions interval: +13 to +37 points), despite A's higher pack reach. These group-level summaries do not tell us who remembered after looking, why B won, or which ad would sell more. Review the shots at the brand reveal, then test a specific change if there is evidence that A's brand cue was hard to link to the claim. If the outcome difference were too uncertain to support a worthwhile gain, report no clear winner rather than breaking the tie with pack reach.
