# RealEye Neuromarketing Report Methodology

Eye tracking answers **what got a look, how soon, and for how long**. It records overt looking, not all attention: a viewer can notice something peripherally or allocate attention without moving their eyes. A hot region might be interesting or difficult to process. Gaze does not by itself establish liking, comprehension, memory, or purchase. Use it to diagnose where to improve a creative; use a separately measured outcome to choose a version.

Platform fields and exports are defined in [RealEye data and metric definitions](realeye-data.md). See the [creative interpretation guide](interpretation.md) for design hypotheses and the [worked examples](worked-example.md) for report wording.

## 1. Define what "best" means

For toothpaste awareness, a useful primary outcome is the percentage of viewers who can link **the correct brand and main benefit** after one exposure. "Brand X, for sensitivity relief" qualifies if that is the tested claim; "a toothpaste ad with a dentist" does not. For a trial goal, prefer an observed product-choice task or a field measure of trial, rather than treating stated purchase intent as actual behavior.

Before testing, specify one primary outcome, a scoring rule and participant denominator, the smallest worthwhile change, and any guardrails (e.g. no material decline in claim clarity). Recall, intent and liking can all be reported, but do not switch to a different deciding metric or construct a recall-plus-intent score after seeing the results. Searching across many AOIs, windows, segments and outcomes invites false positives. Treat unplanned patterns as exploratory, even if a large-looking difference appears.

## 2. Compare under the same conditions

- **Assign one version per person.** Randomly assign the intended audience to separate versions for a first-impression test. Seeing every ad can teach viewers the brand and message before the second exposure; randomizing order alone does not remove that carry-over. If a repeat-view design was already fielded, disclose the limitation, account for order, and use paired analysis when appropriate.
- **Match the placement.** Use the context that matters: an ad break, a social feed, or toothpaste packs on a shelf beside competitors. Keep surrounding content, task, device and relevant rendering conditions comparable across versions; avoid asking people to find the tested claim before measuring spontaneous notice or recall. An isolated ad on a blank screen removes competition and can overstate attention opportunity.
- **Match the exposure.** Pilot an appropriate brief exposure for print or social (a few seconds can be a reasonable first-glance test, not a universal rule). Play the actual video cut at its natural length, with the audio, captions and device expected in that placement. If the placement is skippable, allow realistic skipping in the test where supported and log it separately from gaze. A forced full view cannot estimate real-world skip behavior.
- **Plan the usable sample.** Rough starting points are **30-50 usable people per version** for descriptive heatmaps and **often 100+ per version** for meaningful A/B differences, sometimes substantially more. [NN/g's heatmap study](https://media.nngroup.com/media/reports/free/How_to_Conduct_Eyetracking_Studies.pdf) suggested about 30 usable viewers per map in its setup, not a power rule for ad tests. Plan from the primary metric's expected base rate or variance, worthwhile effect, desired power or precision, and response/quality losses. Webcam noise and smaller AOIs can increase uncertainty for gaze metrics. Do not equate repeated looks or trials with independent people, or assume these counts guarantee stability or adequate power.

## 3. Mark the areas that matter and read them together

For a toothpaste ad, label the brand mark, pack shot (the tube), main claim ("whitening" or "sensitivity relief"), face or smile, and CTA or offer. For a video, add a demonstration or animation if it matters. Use equivalent semantic regions across versions and match the actual boundaries and windows. A tiny logo printed on the pack may not be separable from the tube with webcam tracking; report pack attention rather than overstating logo precision.

| Metric or visual | What it answers | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Fixation Ratio (reach) on brand, pack or claim | What percentage of eligible viewers registered a qualifying fixation? Usually start here for brand visibility. Report `n/N` if known and note the window. | A miss means no recorded fixation, not that the person was unaware of the element. Brand recall or attribution needs its own measure. |
| Time to first fixation (TTFF) | How soon did fixators first reach it? Report alongside reach. Distinguish time from stimulus onset from time relative to AOI visibility. | Fast TTFF among a small subset does not show most people found it, nor that it was their first look overall. Layout, starting position and task affect TTFF. |
| Total fixation time, fixation count, and revisits | How much recorded looking or return activity did an element receive? Compare conditional time among fixators with reach. Optionally compare the brand/claim's **share of recorded on-stimulus fixation time** versus the rest (same window, verified denominator, no double-counted overlap). | Dwell may reflect interest or effort/confusion. Counts or returns do not establish information extraction, motivation, preference, or what sells. Counts also change with sample size, filter and exposure. |
| Mean fixation duration and first fixation duration | How long were individual pauses, or the first qualifying pause, under this filter? Inspect spread if available. | Distributions can be skewed and filter-dependent. A long pause is not a validated measure of depth, liking, or cognitive load. |
| Scanpath, heatmap and view/fog map | Time-ordered fixations show where people went next; maps summarize where recorded activity clustered. Use matched visual settings and show individual paths for examples, not as the group path. | Aggregate maps hide audience differences; color is not time, percent seen or sequence. Web text scanning patterns are descriptive, not ad design rules. |
| Measured VAI | RealEye's per-AOI 0-100 summary of Reach, Speed and Hold. Compare matched windows and explain the components with the underlying metrics. | Not a share of gaze, a recall score, or a creative-selection criterion. The native report's Speed ordering is not the observed path. |

A common hypothesis is that a face or smile captures attention without leading into the product or claim. Test it using matched windows and observed paths. If justified, try redirecting the model's gaze toward the pack, moving the claim nearer the pack, or simplifying one competing element. Do not diagnose distraction just because the face is hot, or default to enlarging the logo without considering integration of pack, brand and copy.

## 4. For video, analyze the moments that carry the message

RealEye's [Images and Videos study type](https://www.realeye.io/support/comparing-study-types) supports video items and follow-up surveys. [Item settings](https://www.realeye.io/support/items-settings) document finish-on-click or finish-on-keypress with an optional minimum display time; use only the behavior actually configured and recorded. Plan a short shot timeline giving the brand reveal, pack reveal and claim/demonstration their own windows. Draw AOI parts for the time and location where the elements are visible, or use verified moving AOIs if the setup supports them. Do not assume automatic object tracking. The image AOI VAI Report is not the video workflow.

| Question | Comparison |
|---|---|
| Did viewers fixate the brand early enough? | Use a shared placement deadline (e.g. first 3 s from playback onset). Show the cohort that started each version, who was still exposed, and the analyzable gaze base. An early skip is an exposure outcome, not a tracking-quality exclusion. |
| When the pack, brand, or claim appears, does it draw the eye? | Pair reach with TTFF relative to each reveal, among those with a verified opportunity to see it; TTFF still describes only fixators. Report actual window bounds and expose any difference in who reached the shot. Use relative metrics only when the time origin and eligibility match the intended reveal. |
| What competes during that shot? | Compare matched-window pack and claim fixation measures with face, animation, or demonstration. Use time slices and participant replays where available to see what happened next. One whole-video map can superimpose unrelated shots and hide a missed reveal. |
| When did attention spread or viewing stop? | Use comparable time bins to describe gaze concentration or spread among valid viewers still watching. Separately report playback/skip drop-off with its starting base and censoring or missingness if relevant. Do not equate missing webcam gaze, a gaze off the stimulus, and stopping playback. |
| Did the message land? | Use brand-and-benefit recall or the chosen outcome. Time spent on the claim is not evidence that it was understood. |

## 5. Ask a short post-view survey

Immediately after the exposure and **before** replay, showing the stimulus, or giving answer choices, ask two open-ended questions:

1. **Unaided brand recall:** "What brand was advertised?"
2. **Unaided message recall:** "What was the main message or benefit?"
3. Then, if relevant, **purchase intent** on a pre-specified scale or **product preference/choice** with stated alternatives. In a one-ad-per-person test, do not show the other ad to ask for a creative preference before measuring recall. Liking and clarity are optional follow-ups, not substitutes for recall or behavior.

Use the [RealEye Survey module](https://www.realeye.io/support/survey-module) or a matched external survey. Turn off the option to display the item above unaided questions. Before seeing outcomes, write accepted spellings and claim paraphrases, how to score a wrong brand, multiple claims, "don't know" and missing answers, and how to handle nonresponse. Score **both correct in the same respondent** for joint recall, not the average or product of separate percentages. If feasible, code text without knowing the respondent's version. Report assigned, exposed, responding and scored counts, and any exclusions by version. A gaze-quality exclusion does not automatically invalidate an otherwise eligible survey response.

## 6. Decide, explain, and retest

Compare the planned outcome with count, denominator, absolute difference (percentage points for rates), and participant-level uncertainty as appropriate for the design. Select a version only for the stated objective when the estimate supports a practically worthwhile gain and any preset guardrails are met. If recall and intent disagree, show the trade-off rather than changing the goal after the fact. If the difference is too uncertain, the data are not comparable, or the outcome is missing, report that there is no clear winner. A hotter map or higher VAI should not break a tie.

| Pattern | Defensible decision or follow-up |
|---|---|
| A gets more recorded looks but B has credibly better joint recall | Choose B for communication in this test. Examine A's dentist or animation during the reveal to develop a testable explanation, not to assert the cause of the difference. |
| High pack reach but poor brand recall | Test a clearer brand name or say it aloud at the pack reveal. Making the tube larger is just one possible edit. |
| High claim reach but poor message recall | Test whether copy, linking it to the product, or presentation timing is the blocker. "Weak message" is a hypothesis, not a deduction from dwell. |
| High liking but weak brand fixation reach | Possible branding visibility problem. Verify attribution using recall, then test a better integrated brand cue. Liking does not fix a visibility gap. |
| Outcome too close to call or not measured | Report the difference and uncertainty, or say it is unavailable. Use gaze to prioritize an edit or decide what to measure next, not to name a winner. |

Report the primary outcome first, then a short scorecard of matched AOI windows, a measured heatmap or shot/replay evidence if available, the method and limitations, and the first edit to test. Distinguish group-level coexistence (e.g. the ad has high pack reach and low recall) from a participant-level relationship, which requires verified joins. Label descriptive attention and self-reported intent honestly; a claim about clicks, purchases or sales needs corresponding behavioral measures.

## RealEye quality and optional signals

- **Tracker and devices:** Document the [actual engine and device](https://www.realeye.io/support/realeye-webcam-v1-vs-v3), calibration, and any coverage or data-loss problems. Webcam v3 is the desktop-oriented default with a four-point calibration; v1 uses device-specific calibration and is required for RealEye facial coding. Mobile fielding selects v1 by default; confirm the actual selection. Compare desktop and mobile separately when device mix or rendered size can affect fixations. Lighting, posture, sampling and calibration all matter.
- **Quality exclusions:** [Item Quality Grade](https://www.realeye.io/support/participant-quality-stats-explained) is a 1-6 summary of sampling, data integrity and gaze on screen. Low sampling (around or below the documented 20 Hz fixation requirement) can leave gaze samples but no usable fixations. Interpolation does not add new measurements. Choose justified, consistent filters before comparing results; [grade 5+](https://www.realeye.io/support/downloading-aoi-related-csv-files-filtered-by-the-item-quality-grade) is an available filter, not a universal requirement. Report exclusions and test sensitivity when appropriate.
- **Geometry and windows:** RealEye's [published accuracy figures](https://www.realeye.io/support/how-to-get-accurate-results) (about 106 px on computers and 56 px on smartphones; an older desktop whitepaper reported about 113 px) are vendor validation estimates, not individual error bounds or cross-device correction factors. Its [5-10% AOI size guidance](https://www.realeye.io/support/areas-of-interest-aois) is a rule of thumb relative to image area, not proof of separability. For images, native cumulative windows are nominally 0.5-3 s, 0.5-5 s and 0.5-10 s, limited by exposure. A 0.5 s start reduces influence of initial center looks but does not remove all position bias or describe those omitted looks.
- **Fixation settings:** Record actual export settings and algorithm version instead of assuming defaults. [RealEye's I-VT-like filter](https://www.realeye.io/support/fixation-filter) uses rendered CSS displacement and an estimated visual angle. Its documented defaults include a 200 ms noise-reduction window, 80/400 ms min/max fixation durations, 30 estimated degrees/s velocity and 150 ms max saccade duration. Changing these settings can change count, duration and reach; use the same settings for compared results. For scrollable items, distinguish overall reach from reach conditional on visibility.
- **K-coefficient, expressions and pupils:** Optional signals, not baseline report requirements. [Coefficient K](https://doi.org/10.1145/2896452) compares standardized fixation duration with subsequent saccade amplitude; positive values reflect relatively longer fixations and shorter saccades (focal), negative the reverse (ambient). Check whether values are from a normalized plot or raw CSV. Neither sign establishes memory encoding or confusion. [RealEye facial coding](https://www.realeye.io/support/facial-coding) provides expression scores (smile, surprise, neutral) only when collected in v1; FACS describes facial movements, not proven felt emotions. Speech or movement can confound it. Pupil changes can reflect luminance and other causes. Require separate validation and corroborating outcomes before interpreting any of these as arousal or emotion.

## Evidence notes and reading list

Use these sources for the specific point supported by the study design. Publisher records and abstracts establish scope, not every downstream inference. Commercial examples illustrate workflow, not norms or proof of effect.

### Foundations and limits

- [Holmqvist, Nyström, Andersson et al. (2011), *Eye Tracking: A Comprehensive Guide to Methods and Measures*](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/eye-tracking-9780199697083) is a handbook for fixations, saccades, AOIs and event-detection methods. [Holmqvist et al. (2023)](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/32467/) propose an evidence-based minimal reporting guideline. [Wedel and Pieters (2008), *Eye Tracking for Visual Marketing*](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:now:fntmkt:1700000011) reviews visual attention, the eye and visual brain, measurement and marketing applications. [Jacob and Karn (2003), in *The Mind's Eye*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/edited-volume/abs/pii/B9780444510204500311) surveys eye tracking in HCI, both for usability research and as an input method.
- [Orquin and Holmqvist (2018), *Threats to the validity of eye-movement research in psychology*](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-017-0998-z) identifies nine validity threats across design, preprocessing, analysis and interpretation, including selecting measures after seeing results. [Tatler (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1167/7.14.4) documents central fixation bias in scene viewing. [Greene, Liu and Wolfe (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2012.03.019) could not reliably decode viewers' instructed tasks from static scanpaths; [Borji and Itti (2014)](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24665092) found modest above-chance decoding with different features and a classifier. Treat these as separate studies, not as citations from Teixeira's earlier video-ad paper. They do not license inferring motives from an individual path.
- The [2022 *Eye Movement and Pupil Measures* review](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomp.2021.733531) distinguishes metric definitions and discusses multiple influences on dwell and pupil measures. [An overt/covert attention experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9232469/) demonstrates allocation without eye movements in its task. [A study of fixation duration in subtitled videos](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8012014/) cautions against relying on a single mean for skewed durations; its learning-task findings are not ad thresholds. [Bylinskii et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.03605) compare metrics for scoring saliency predictions against gaze. That is a model evaluation question, not evidence that a predicted heatmap measures real participants.
- **Choice prediction is task-specific.** [Papadopoulos et al. (ETRA 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24849v1#S3.SS2) studied 30 people and 1,800 repeated, self-paced lab trials comparing AI-generated image pairs. Gaze shifted toward the chosen image about a second before response; chosen images received more dwell, fixations and revisits. A classifier **excluding last-fixation location** reached **67.6%** versus a **50%** baseline with five-fold cross-validation grouped by participant. The paper reports more switching in lower-confidence trials. This is one controlled choice task, not spontaneous ad viewing or validation of a predictor for preferences, ad recall, or sales in a new audience.

### Screen and mobile attention

- [NN/g's updated F-pattern article](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/) describes two horizontal passes and a vertical stem in the **reading area**, not navigation. It is a fallback when text and task invite scanning without strong cues, not a required pattern. NN/g observed it on mobile, mirrored for right-to-left text, and describes marking (eyes in place while scrolling) as more common on mobile. Its [text scanning article](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/text-scanning-patterns-eyetracking/) lists F, spotted, layer-cake and commitment patterns in increasing effectiveness for that text-reading context. These articles come from the same practitioner group; do not count them as independent replications or use the ranking to judge an ad.
- [Simola et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024042) compared reading and browsing on web pages. In the reported experiments animated or abruptly appearing ads could capture overt attention and disrupt reading; browsing drew more ad fixations than the reading task. This motivates matching the task, not assuming motion always helps an ad.
- [Zhu et al. (2022), *Attention allocation on mobile app interfaces*](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35847542) examined fixation durations as interface presentation changed. The [EurekAlert brief](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/842238) was published in 2020, not 2025; its [original Leiva et al. mobile UI study](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/understanding-visual-saliency-in-mobile-user-interfaces/) found weaker contributions from size/color than expected in that dataset. Neither provides a rule that bigger or brighter elements always win or always fail. Treat comparative vendor observations (including a reported absence of an EyeQuant F-bias) as context, not independent evidence for a universal pattern.

### Advertising and commercial use

- [Pieters and Wedel (2004), *Attention Capture and Transfer in Advertising*](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.68.2.36.27794) studied 1,363 print ads with over 3,600 consumers. In those layouts pictorials captured attention beyond what size explained, text attention increased with area, and brand elements were associated with transfer to other elements. This motivates separate brand, picture and copy AOIs, not blanket rules for all designs. [Wedel and Pieters (2000)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormksc:v:19:y:2000:i:4:p:297-312) separately tested brand recognition; pictorial and brand fixations related to recognition in that study, text fixations did not. Fixation count rather than duration as an information-extraction input was a **modeling assumption**, not a universal finding.
- [Pieters and Wedel, *Ad Gist* (2012 issue)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormksc:v:31:y:2012:i:1:p:59-73) measured ad gist under very brief exposure; [Higgins, Leinenger and Rayner's 2014 review](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00210) summarizes gist at about 100 ms in some print tasks and scene fixations averaging around 300 ms depending on scene and task. Do not turn either into a brand-recall or optimal-duration benchmark. [Wedel and Pieters (2015)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormksc:v:34:y:2015:i:1:p:134-143) explore color, typicality and brief or blurred exposure; [Pieters, Wedel and Batra (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.74.5.48) is a source on visual complexity.
- [Teixeira, Wedel and Pieters (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.10.0207) combined facial-expression estimates of surprise and joy, eye-tracked attention concentration, and zapping/retention in online video ads. These were distinct measures and their modeled relationships differed; do not use gaze alone to infer either emotion or retention. [Chandon et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.73.6.1) found that shelf position effects on brand attention and evaluation were not interchangeable.
- [Yu, Xie and Chen (2018)](https://doi.org/10.16538/j.cnki.fem.2018.12.007) review applications in advertising beyond static print, food packaging, web design, product design and shelf layout. The [2024 MSI working paper *Predicting Attention to Advertising*](https://thearf-org-unified-admin.s3.amazonaws.com/MSI_Report_24-110.pdf) models gaze from ad features and optimizes placement against predicted gaze. Treat the cited version as working-paper evidence, not proof of consumer outcomes.
- **Practitioner sources:** Tobii Pro Lab and [Tobii XR metrics and interpretation](https://developer.tobii.com/xr/learn/analytics/fundamentals/analysis-and-interpretation/) illustrate uses such as comparing time to find a logo after a redesign; verify platform-specific definitions. [RealEye's *Visual Attention Metrics That Drive Better Decisions*](https://insights.realeye.io/visual-attention-metrics), its [Fruvita case](https://www.realeye.io/blog/post/realeye-in-neuromarketing-a-case-study-with-fruvita), the [iMotions metric glossary](https://imotions.com/blog/learning/10-terms-metrics-eye-tracking/), and [Smashing Magazine's 2021 mobile UX article](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2021/10/eye-tracking-mobile-ux-research/) are useful for reporting practice and examples, not peer-reviewed evidence or benchmarks.

### What not to promote to conclusions

- Saliency does not imply interest, dwell does not measure motivation, revisits do not prove interest, and longer individual fixations do not prove deeper processing. These were unsupported as general mappings in the supplied research notes; task-specific associations can still be studied. Driving glance-transition research and line-level fixation assignment in reading do not establish attentiveness in ads or mobile UI.
- A [webcam and EyeLink comparison](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11289017/) measured different spatial errors for its equipment and tasks. A precise claim that webcam effect sizes are **20-27% smaller** than lab effects was not verified. Do not use it to correct reports, set sample size, or generalize across trackers.
- The reviewed studies and vendor cases do not establish how widely agencies use these methods, or that gaze alone reliably predicts recall, clicks, purchases or sales for a new creative. Controlled memory, preference and retention studies show specific relationships only. Many studies use small samples, laboratory settings or narrowly defined tasks. Preserve that scope and measure the actual outcome when it drives the decision.
