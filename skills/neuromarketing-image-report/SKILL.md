# Neuromarketing image report

Use **measured gaze to diagnose the creative**, not to pick the best ad by itself. Attention tells you where people looked, not what they liked, understood, remembered, or will buy. For a creative choice, pair eye tracking with an independent outcome: unaided brand and claim recall for communication, or a product-choice or field test for trial. If no such outcome was measured, give attention findings and a retest plan, not an effectiveness winner.

Read the [RealEye Neuromarketing Report Methodology](<references/RealEye Neuromarketing Report Methodology.md>) before planning or adjudicating a comparison. It contains the survey rubric, video workflow, research notes, and evidence limits.

## 1. Identify the decision

- Capture the exact creative version, target audience, placement (feed, ad break, print or shelf), task (free viewing or directed search), exposure, and the decision the brand team needs to make. Keep tested conditions separate from the intended real-world placement.
- Before testing, specify **one primary outcome**, its scoring and denominator, the smallest worthwhile difference, and any guardrails. Example: *percentage of respondents who can name the toothpaste brand and give its whitening claim without prompting, in the same response record*. Count other outcomes and attention measures as secondary or diagnostic. Do not search many AOIs, windows and audiences and promote whichever result looks best. If no outcome was specified before the data were seen, label the comparison exploratory.
- Define the must-not-miss elements and when they matter. For a toothpaste ad, start with the logo, pack shot, claim or demonstration, face/smile, and CTA or offer. State what the edit must preserve.

## 2. Establish what evidence is available

Read [RealEye data and metric definitions](references/realeye-data.md) before extracting numbers.

| Input | What to deliver |
|---|---|
| Dashboard access (signed in), AOI statistics, AOI VAI report, or exports | VAI, Reach, Speed and Hold per AOI, window and audience. See the [HTML report guide](references/html-report.md) for how to pull them. |
| Survey or choice outcome, with a variant mapping | Assess the planned outcome if the design and data support it. Without gaze, do not infer why viewers responded differently. With gaze, report both measures and their separate denominators. |
| Only a measured heatmap | Qualitative clusters and possible blind spots with the map's window and filters if known. No numbers read from colours. |
| Creative alone, no usable results, or a link that requires unavailable access | No measured findings. Request the AOI statistics or AOI VAI report with the matching creative. Request the survey data if asked to pick a winner. If the creative is available, offer an AOI plan labelled as hypotheses. Do not substitute Attention Predictor. |

**Collect the data with the browser** by following [the browser playbook](references/browser-data-collection.md): it explains every step, from the ids to the VAI numbers, AOI shapes, clean image, heatmaps and the AOI VAI report. Use supplied files first. A `app.realeye.io/dashboard/item/...` URL is a web UI link, not a public API endpoint. Use authorised dashboard access (the user's signed-in browser) for read-only inspection. On a login wall, ask the user to sign in or to supply exports. Do not upload private creatives or participant exports to outside services. Do not ask for passwords or tokens in chat, edit saved AOIs, change participant status, or create share links. Never print participant names or other personal data.

## 3. Design or audit the comparison

1. **Assignment and placement.** For a first-impression A/B test, randomly assign target viewers to **one version each**; seeing A can teach the brand or claim before B. Match context, task, device, and exposure conditions: a real or credible feed, ad break, or shelf with competitors where possible. Use placement-appropriate timing (a few seconds for a brief feed exposure; actual runtime, audio and realistic skip conditions for video). An isolated forced view can overstate real-world attention opportunity.
2. **Study and sample.** Match the exact item/version and record the analysed base per item/window, sample source, tracker, filters and exclusions. Rough planning only: **30-50 usable viewers per version** for descriptive maps; detecting meaningful A/B differences can need **100+ per version**; **30+ per audience** for gender and age cuts. Size the study for the primary outcome, expected variance, smallest worthwhile effect, and losses.
3. **Regions.** Verify saved AOIs on the tested creative, including size and overlap, and name each AOI by what it covers on the image. Prefer regions the tracker can separate: webcam tracking is less precise than a lab tracker. Label AOIs chosen after seeing results exploratory. Do not fabricate measurements.
4. **Windows.** Use the first 3, 5 and 10 seconds (cumulative, starting at 0.5 s to reduce initial centre bias) or the placement-relevant equivalents, and state the actual bounds. For **video**, map AOIs by shot or with validated moving regions if supported. Compare attention by a shared deadline (e.g. the first 3 s) and from the actual brand/pack/claim reveal among people who reached that moment. Inspect time-sliced maps, not a single whole-video heatmap.

## 4. Answer the questions behind the brief

Use [the interpretation guide](references/interpretation.md) to choose which questions matter. Lead with the evidence that could change the decision. Answer them with **VAI and its components only**:

- **Was the key element reached?** Reach: the share of viewers who looked at the AOI. A low Reach is *few viewers looked there in this window*, not *nobody saw it*.
- **Was it noticed soon enough?** Speed: how early in the viewing order. Rank the AOIs by Speed to get the viewing order.
- **Did viewers stay?** Hold: how long attention stays on the AOI relative to the window. Short Hold on a logo can be fine; on a claim that must be read it is not.
- **Did the intended hierarchy work?** Compare the VAI of brand, message, product, action and competing elements according to their roles, then explain each with its Reach, Speed and Hold. VAI is neither a share of the image's attention nor a percentage of viewers, and a higher VAI does not necessarily serve the goal.

For each finding: cite the **observation** (AOI, VAI components, audience, window, N), explain its implication, and separate the **design hypothesis**. A direct-gaze face with higher Hold and a weaker brand banner may justify testing model gaze direction; it does not show the face caused the miss. Heatmaps average people and omit covert attention.

## 5. Score the outcome before choosing

- Immediately after one exposure, ask **"What brand was advertised?"** and **"What was the main message?"** as unaided, open-ended questions. Do not show the creative again or give answer cues before recall. Agree on accepted brand spellings and claim paraphrases before reading variant results. Count joint recall only when the **same respondent** gets both right.
- If appropriate, add purchase intent (a scale) or product preference/choice after recall; optionally ask clarity or liking. Self-reported intent is not trial or sales. For a trial or behavioural goal, use a measured product choice or field outcome rather than inferring it from a survey or gaze.
- Compare the preselected outcome by version, with counts, analysed bases, absolute differences and uncertainty at the participant level when estimable. Respect preset guardrails; do not combine recall and intent after seeing scores. Prefer B for a communication goal if it has credibly better joint recall, even if A has higher VAI. If outcome data are missing, the evidence is too close to call, or the guardrail is unresolved, say so. Do not break a tie with VAI or a heatmap.
- Use discrepancies to plan the edit. High pack Reach but poor brand recall can motivate a clearer or spoken brand cue at the pack reveal; high claim Reach but poor takeout can motivate rewriting the message. High liking and weak brand Reach can flag a branding risk, not demonstrate failed attribution.

## 6. Deliver the report

Write in plain language. **The deliverable is a self-contained, professional HTML report** (one file, images embedded, dark mode, print-to-PDF ready) built with the bundled script `scripts/build_report.py`. Use another format only if the user asks. See [the HTML report guide](references/html-report.md) for the JSON schema, how to pull the data from the RealEye dashboard, and the heatmap sheet generator; see the [worked example](references/worked-example.md) for tone.

### Standing rules for every report

**Write the main report for brand managers.** It exists to help them choose and act, so the main sections are plain, short and free of technical detail. Anything technical goes in a **Technical appendix at the end** (rule 3). In the main sections say "viewers" and "elements" (not AOIs, N, windows, filters or trackers), lead with what to do, and keep a one-line footnote only where a reader could misread a number (e.g. † marks a small audience).

1. **VAI only.** Use only RealEye's VAI and its components Reach, Speed and Hold. Never show raw metrics such as time to first fixation (TTFF), fixation time, fixation ratio, gaze or fixation counts, revisits or K-coefficient: on their own they mislead (a fast TTFF for a handful of viewers looks like speed; a long dwell can mean reading difficulty). The script does not render them.
2. **Say how it was measured, in one plain line near the top:** the test was run on the **RealEye platform with webcam eye tracking** (viewers saw the images on their own devices and their gaze was recorded through their webcams). The details (tracker precision, sample, filters) go in the appendix.
3. **Structure.** Main sections, in this order: *The designs* (every whole design with what it presents and its assumed purpose) → the one-line how-it-was-measured note → *Overall findings and conclusions* (verdict, findings each in its own proven sub-section, conclusions, what to do next) → *Gender and age insights* → *Hypotheses tested* → *Tested designs and the elements analysed*, the study facts tiles and *Who took part* → *What the scores mean (VAI)* (plain definition of Reach, Speed, Hold, how to use it, component patterns, a worked example) → *Viewing order* → one section per design → head-to-head comparison → how to test it next → *How to improve the study*. Then the **Technical appendix**: about this study (platform, tracking, sample, participant quality filter, windows, audiences, areas of interest, tracker precision), study and platform facts, report and skill version, technical notes on VAI (cumulative windows, compare like with like, small groups), evidence and limits, what would change the recommendation, definitions (linked to RealEye support), sources, and the unfiltered dashboard AOI VAI report; then the *Study blueprint* and the closing *AI-agent prompt*. `scripts/audit_report.py` checks this order.
4. **A section that explains VAI,** in plain language, in the main sections: Reach, Speed and Hold in one line each, that VAI is for comparing (not a percentage of viewers), that a higher score means the element is better at attracting visual attention (judged against its role), that it shows attention not outcome, and the component patterns table.
5. **Clear viewing order.** Show the order of the elements (ranked by VAI Speed) for every design and every window, say whether it is stable across the first 3, 5 and 10 seconds, show numbered markers on the image, and list which elements most viewers reach in each window. It is a relative hierarchy across viewers, never one person's scan path.
6. **Quality filter (technical, appendix).** Analyse only viewers with data quality **Average or better** (`participantQualityGradeItemMin=3&participantQualityGradeItemMax=6` in the dashboard). Apply it to every table, heatmap and audience cut. State the analysed N and how many tests it removed in the appendix. If a source cannot be filtered (the dashboard *AOI VAI report* ignores filters), label it as unfiltered and keep it out of the primary results.
7. **Audiences and windows in full.** For every design give VAI and its components for **all viewers, gender (men, women), age bands (e.g. 18–24, 25–34, 35+) and every gender × age combination, each in the first 3, 5 and 10 seconds**, plus a **heatmap for every audience and window**. Show N per audience, mark groups under 100 viewers ‡ (indicative) and under 30 †, muting the latter, and say plainly when a gap is within noise. Viewers with other or unspecified gender or no recorded age are in the overall results but not in these cuts: say so (appendix).
8. **Embed the dashboard AOI VAI report** per design (analysed image with its AOIs, executive summary, AOI overview, components, per-window tables, insights) in the technical appendix, labelled as unfiltered.
9. **Clean, complete images.** Show the whole creative (tall images have content below the visible viewport), never a mouse pointer on a heatmap or overlay (move the pointer off the image before capturing). Name elements by what they cover (the brand name banner, not the label printed on the pack).
10. **Never state a number you did not read.** Every value comes from RealEye data with its audience and window. Check that VAI equals the mean of its three components before using a row.
11. **Link every table and heatmap to the RealEye dashboard.** Wherever data or a heatmap is shown, give a link to the same view in the RealEye dashboard (heatmap, AOI report, AOI VAI report), with the audience's gender, age and quality filters applied in the link. Always set `dashboard.study_id` and each design's `item_id` in the report JSON; the script then adds the links to the design headers, score tables, audience column headers, every heatmap, the viewing order, the comparison and the appendix.
12. **Make it look like the example, and like the RealEye dashboard.** Every report must look and read like the bundled example (`assets/example/example-report.html`): same section order, same plain-language tone, same tables, charts and heatmap sheets. The design follows the RealEye dashboard's AOI VAI report: light grey surface, white square cards, Barlow type, orange accent, uppercase labels, stat tiles, a RealEye top bar, outlined AOIs on the clean image with a *Show AOIs* switch, AOI cards, best/lowest-value pills and component bars (VAI orange, Reach blue, Speed yellow, Hold green). Start from the template (`assets/template/report.template.json`), fill every placeholder, and do not change the layout or add your own styling; the script owns the look.
13. **Use the clean image and the real AOI shapes.** Capture the clean analysed image (no outlines, no pointer) and give each AOI its polygon (`shape`, in % of the image) and dashboard colour; the script draws the outlines, the numbered viewing order and the AOI cards from them. Get the shapes from the dashboard's AOI endpoint (see the guide).
14. **Check how it renders.** Before delivery render the HTML to an image and look at it (for example `msedge --headless=new --user-data-dir=<temp> --window-size=1200,2600 --screenshot=out.png file:///report.html`), and fix anything cut off, overlapping or unreadable. Then run `python scripts/audit_report.py report.html --json report.json`: every check must pass.

15. **Show the viewing order clearly, overall and by audience.** The report must visualise the viewing order three ways: numbered badges on the AOI corners of the image, a ladder of numbered steps with Speed bars for each time window, and a table for every audience (all viewers, men, women, each age band, each gender × age) and window listing the elements 1st to 5th with differences from the overall order outlined. The script builds all three from the audience VAI values; give every audience (`segments`) so none is missing.
16. **Make every design mention show its thumbnail.** Wherever a design is mentioned ("Design A", "A" in a table or chart legend, a badge), hovering it, focusing it or clicking it shows a small thumbnail of that design with its name, so readers always know which is A and which is B. The script does this automatically for design badges, tags and legends. In free text (verdicts, findings, actions, limits, notes) write designs as **"Design A"** or the token **`{A}`** (for example "in {A} and {B}"); common patterns such as "(A)", "in A", "A's", "A 14.3" and "A: …" are linked automatically. Do not leave a bare letter for a design elsewhere (a sentence-initial article "A" is fine).

17. **Present the tested images and the AOIs in the intro.** After the findings, gender and age insights and hypotheses (the whole designs are already at the very top), the report must show every tested image in a *Tested designs and the elements analysed* card: the design letter and name, the image with its AOIs outlined (with a *Show AOIs* switch for the clean image), a legend that says what each AOI covers (`aois[].role`, for example "The claim “Wake up the red in your hair.”"), the number of viewers, and links to the design's heatmap, AOI report and AOI VAI report in the RealEye dashboard. Whenever AOIs are analysed there must be at least this one place where the reader sees which part of the design each AOI is; write each `role` as a plain description of the area, not a category.

18. **Explain every metric name with a tooltip.** Wherever a name like VAI, VAI Reach, VAI Speed, VAI Hold, Reach, Speed, Hold or AOI appears in the report (text, table headers, legends, tiles, navigation), hovering or clicking it shows a short plain-language definition. The script adds these tooltips automatically to all report text, so write the names exactly as they are (capitalised: VAI, Reach, Speed, Hold, AOI). If the report uses another term a brand manager might not know, add it to the report's `glossary` (`{"term": "definition"}`) and it gets a tooltip everywhere it appears.

19. **End the report with an AI-agent prompt.** The very last section, *Continue the analysis with an AI agent*, holds a ready-to-use prompt with a *Copy prompt* button and a *Download as .txt* button. The script builds it from the report data, so it is always complete: the study and method, how to read VAI, the designs and AOIs (with what each covers and where), every VAI, Reach, Speed and Hold number for every design, audience and window, the dashboard's AOI VAI report, the findings, actions and limits, the dashboard links, and rules for answering (cite design, audience, window and N; separate observation, interpretation and hypothesis; flag small audiences; never invent metrics; say when the data cannot answer) plus ideas to explore. The agent confirms it has read the report and asks what to explore. Set the optional `agent_context` (the campaign goal, the decision to make, anything the assistant should know) to add context to the prompt. The same section holds a collapsed **data pack** (markdown with tidy CSV tables, generated from the report data): study, designs, elements with their position, audiences and sizes, the main table (design × audience × window × element: VAI, Reach, Speed, Hold, viewing-order rank), the unfiltered dashboard AOI VAI report and the dashboard links, with *Copy data pack*, *Download data pack (.md)* and *Download main table (.csv)* buttons. The prompt includes the pack, so an assistant never has to parse the report. Do not remove or shorten this section.

20. **State the study facts and who took part.** Near the top (tiles) the report must say when the study was created, when the data was collected (first and last date of the analysed viewers) and which RealEye version was used (the dashboard version shown at analysis, since the version at collection time is not recorded; say so). A *Who took part* card follows the tested designs, with charts and counts for each design: gender, age bands (with the share who gave no age), device, number analysed of collected, median age, and the collection dates. The technical appendix adds the study status and type, stimulus settings, eye tracker (the RealEye webcam tracker version), participant type (own participants or RealEye panel), browser window size, and the data-quality distribution of everyone collected with how many were removed. Collect all of it in the browser (see the playbook, section 2b) and put it in `study_info` and `participants` in the report JSON. Never report participant names or study author names. Gender and age are self-reported: say so, and say how many viewers gave none.
21. **Show data as tables and as charts wherever a chart helps.** Every comparison that matters has both: a chart for the pattern and a table for the exact numbers. The script draws VAI component bars, build-up lines over the windows, head-to-head bars, the viewing-order ladder, audience bar charts with first 3 s / 5 s / 10 s tabs (gender, age and gender × age) above their tables, diverging charts for women-minus-men gaps, and participant profile bars. Do not replace a table with a chart or the reverse unless the data is too small to chart; when you add an analysis, add both.
22. **The findings are the most important part of the report, and each one is proven.** *Overall findings and conclusions* is the first analysis section (right after the designs and the how-measured note), visually the strongest, and contains: the verdict, a findings-at-a-glance list, then **one sub-section per finding** (headline, conclusion, *why we conclude this*, evidence, what it means, confidence), then one sub-section per conclusion and per action, each saying which findings it follows from (`based_on`). Every finding needs evidence items, and every evidence item links to the table, chart or heatmap in the report that proves it (`ref`) and to the RealEye dashboard (`dash`), and quotes its numbers as `data` points that the build script looks up and checks. A wrong number, an unknown link target or a finding without why or evidence stops the build: fix the JSON, do not work around it. Per-design findings and gender/age findings are also one sub-section each and link to their tables.
23. **Check the conclusions before you publish them.** Before the build, an agent must (a) re-read every finding as the brand manager would and ask: is it true, is it useful, would it change a decision? Drop findings that only restate a number; (b) test each causal word ("because", "caused", "drives") against the evidence and weaken it to "likely" or "hypothesis" when designs differ in more than one thing; (c) give each finding an honest confidence (high, medium, low) with the sample sizes behind it; (d) check the findings do not contradict each other or the tables; (e) make sure the conclusions answer the user's decision, name what to keep and what to change, and say what attention data cannot show (recall, liking, purchase). Order findings by how much they matter for the decision, not by section order.
24. **The report is a backup of the study.** The *Study blueprint* section holds everything needed to recreate and rerun the study: study set-up (type, stimuli, display time, finish mode, instructions, order, target participants, questions asked), the stimulus images (downloadable), the exact AOI geometry in image pixels (bounding box, vertices for polygons, shape, colour, time range, image size), the analysis settings, step-by-step instructions to run it again with new participants, and downloadable `study-blueprint.json` and `report.json`. The same data is in the data pack (`aoi_coordinates` table). Always collect AOI shapes in pixels or percent, never only a picture of them (playbook section 3), and put the study set-up in `blueprint`.
25. **The report and the skill are versioned.** The version in this file (`metadata.version`) is stamped into every report: the header, the footer, the HTML `generator` meta tag, the technical appendix, the data pack, the study blueprint JSON and the embedded report data. Set `skill_version` in the report JSON to the same version, and keep `CHANGELOG.md` current: every change to the skill bumps the version in this file and adds a changelog entry in the same commit. Do not edit the stamp by hand.

26. **Start with the designs.** Before anything else the report shows each whole design (full image, no AOI outlines) with a few words on *what it presents* and its *assumed purpose* (what the designer wants viewers to notice, do or feel): `variants[].presents` and `variants[].purpose`, both required.
27. **Higher is better, and say so.** A higher VAI, Reach, Speed or Hold means the element is better at attracting visual attention. The report says it at the findings, in the VAI explainer, in the tooltips and under the tables, with the caveat that each element is judged against its role. Never write that a higher score is "not always better" without that context.
28. **Whenever two designs' numbers are quoted, give the difference.** Never leave the reader to subtract. Write `{{cmp:metric:AOI:window:A,B}}` (renders "VAI 61.2 in A and 61.8 in B (B is 0.6 points higher)"), `{{diff:metric:AOI:window:A,B}}` (only "B is 0.6 points higher") or `{{gap:metric:AOI:window:design:Women,Men}}` for two audiences. The script looks the numbers up, so they cannot be mistyped. AOI names used in tokens must not contain a colon. The build warns about unpaired design numbers in prose.
29. **Test the designer's hypotheses.** After the findings and the gender and age insights, a section *Did the design do what it was meant to?* states at least three assumptions the designer or brand owner probably had (`hypotheses[]`), answers each with a verdict (supported, partly supported, not supported, cannot tell), a conclusion, why, and proven evidence, exactly like a finding. Derive them from the design and its assumed purpose; use "cannot tell" for anything attention data cannot show (recall, liking, intent).
30. **Gender and age insights come straight after the overall findings**, then the hypotheses; the detailed analysis follows.
31. **Suggest how to improve the study.** A section *How to improve the study* (`study_improvements[]`) has at least one idea in each of: different or more AOIs, more or different respondents (name the groups and numbers that are too small), a survey (a table of concrete questions with why each helps: unaided brand and message recall, first noticed, purchase intent, appeal, familiarity, choice), and benchmarking against the best-performing similar designs including competitors (own earlier campaigns, category leaders; predicted attention may be used to screen many creatives but must be labelled predicted). Base ideas on what this study could not answer.
32. **Every "RealEye" has a tooltip.** The word RealEye gets a tooltip that says what RealEye is and, outside other links, links to www.RealEye.io. The Definitions in the appendix each link to the matching RealEye support page (`SUPPORT` in `build_report.py`).
33. **A floating menu shows where you are and lets you send links.** A fixed contents menu (a side panel on wide screens, a button on narrow ones) highlights the part being read, jumps to every main part and to each finding and hypothesis, and each entry and each section heading has a button that copies a link to that part (the `#anchor`).
34. **The reader can mark up the report.** A *Mark up* tool lets the reader highlight text in four colours, mark an area (for example on a heatmap) and erase marks. Marks are saved in the reader's browser only and print with the report.

35. **If the design has no AOIs, create useful ones.** First read the item's AOIs. When there are none, define 3 to 8 AOIs yourself, following `references/aoi-design.md`: start from the decision, the designer's purpose and the hypotheses; cover brand, product, claim, offer or call to action, and face or key visual; give each a name, a `role` and a `why`; keep them tight, not tiny (sides of at least about 6% of the image) and not overlapping by accident; use the same AOIs for every design in a comparison. Check them with `scripts/check_aois.py` and look at its preview, measure them read-only with `dashboard_vai_collect.js` (`customAois`), and compare them with the heatmap to catch missed hot spots. Mark them `aoi_source: "proposed"`: the report then says they were defined for it and are not saved in RealEye. Never save or edit AOIs in the user's study unless they ask; the blueprint gives the coordinates to draw them.

36. **Be clear that certainty needs at least 100 viewers per group.** To be certain about a result, every group the client wants to analyse (each design, each gender, each age band, each gender × age cell) needs at least **100 viewers** with Average or better data quality. Groups of 30 to 99 are *indicative* (marked ‡) and groups under 30 *anecdotal* (marked †). The report states this rule near the top, lists which groups reach 100 and which do not (per design), repeats it above the audience tables and in the appendix, and marks every group's N with the marker. A finding or hypothesis whose evidence uses a group under 100 can not have 'high' confidence (the build fails; a `weak-evidence` finding that is about the small group itself is exempt), and its confidence note must name the group sizes. Never call a result certain when a group is under 100; say what sample would make it certain. Always ask in the brief which groups the client wants to analyse, and recommend how many more viewers each needs (`study_improvements`, retest plan).

37. **Heatmaps illustrate; the data decides.** Heatmaps are only a picture of the gaze data: how one looks depends on its colour scale, smoothing and each map's own scaling, and the strength of two maps cannot be compared. The report says so at every heatmap and in the findings and the VAI explainer. The analysis and every conclusion must rest on the numbers (VAI and its Reach, Speed, Hold), never on how a heatmap looks: do not write findings such as "the heatmap is hotter here", every finding and hypothesis needs evidence items with `data` points (the build fails otherwise), and a heatmap-only evidence item is tagged *Illustration only*. Use a heatmap to illustrate a number or to spot a missed element (rule 35), then confirm it with VAI.

### Build the report

1. **Start from the template.** Copy `assets/template/report.template.json` into a new working folder and open `assets/example/example-report.html` (built from `assets/example/report.json`) as the model of how the finished report should look and read. The example is a real two-design report; match its structure, level of detail and tone.
2. Pull VAI, Reach, Speed and Hold for each audience × window (`scripts/dashboard_vai_collect.js`) and the heatmap sheets (`scripts/dashboard_heatmap_sheets.js`), plus the AOI overlay image and the dashboard's own heatmap, following the [guide](references/html-report.md). Name the image files as in the template.
3. Fill the template (every `<…>` placeholder; set `dashboard.study_id` and each `item_id` so the links work).
4. Run `python scripts/build_report.py report.json -o report.html`. The script draws the charts, viewing order, audience tables, comparison and the RealEye links. Then run `python scripts/audit_report.py report.html --json report.json` and fix until every check passes.
5. Open the HTML, check that it renders (no pointer on images, nothing cut off, links open the right dashboard view) and that the numbers match the source, then hand the file to the user. Keep several designs (each analysed separately, then compared) in one report.

Before delivery, reconcile numbers and units with the source, make sure the audience, window and N travel with each number, and remove person-level data, private access links and credentials from the shareable report. Do not publish or send the report without permission.
